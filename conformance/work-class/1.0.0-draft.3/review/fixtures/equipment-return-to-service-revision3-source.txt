### 01-request-and-scope.txt
Business record: return-to-service request

Record ID: RTS-REQ-417
Issued by: Shift Maintenance Coordinator, Northstar Plant Operations Unit NPO-7
Request date: 2026-09-12

The trigger is an authorized inspection and approval for equipment asset EQ-204, a synthetic pump skid, under work order WO-7304. Inspection record IN-552 says the inspection is complete. Approval record AP-091 records approval by the Reliability Supervisor. The intended result is one update in the Maintenance Register showing EQ-204 as returned to service for WO-7304.

The ordered in-class path is: first validate the authority, boundaries, preconditions, and pre-operation gate; then submit one return-to-service entry to the Maintenance Register; then read the native completion record and verify its required values. The exact external action is one “Return to Service” transaction in the Maintenance Register service. The values sent with it are asset EQ-204, work order WO-7304, inspection IN-552, approval AP-091, effective time 2026-09-12 14:30 UTC, performer role Maintenance Records Clerk, and reason RTS-AUTH-INSPECTED. The path ends after the native completion record has been checked.

Participants are the Shift Maintenance Coordinator who submits the request, the Inspection Engineer who owns IN-552, the Reliability Supervisor who grants or withdraws authority, the Maintenance Records Clerk who performs the gate and register entry, the Maintenance Register service that records the entry, and the Maintenance Records Custodian who owns register corrections. The principal represented by the request is Northstar Plant Operations Unit NPO-7.

Maintenance Control Standard MCS-12, revision 4, declares this record update to have medium business materiality and medium reversibility. It changes a maintenance record that informs dispatch and maintenance-history decisions, so the result matters to later business decisions. It is reversible only through a separately authorized correction entry linked to the original record. It does not start, stop, move, energize, isolate, or otherwise control EQ-204 or any other equipment. Physical equipment control, an inspection, an approval, a safety release, and a correction are outside this in-class operation and must be handled under their own procedures.

### 02-authority-and-validity.txt
Business record: authority and validity for RTS-REQ-417

The permission is asserted on behalf of Northstar Plant Operations Unit NPO-7. The Reliability Supervisor grants permission. The Maintenance Records Clerk performs the permitted operation. The permitted operation is recording return to service for equipment EQ-204 in its Maintenance Register record, using work order WO-7304, inspection IN-552, and approval AP-091. The permission covers one register update for that exact asset, work order, inspection, and approval combination. It cannot be reused for another asset, work order, inspection, approval, or later work instance.

Before the entry, IN-552 must be complete, AP-091 must identify EQ-204 and WO-7304, no open safety hold may exist for EQ-204, and the Maintenance Records Clerk must be on duty, authenticated, and occupying the Maintenance Records Clerk role. The Reliability Supervisor must have been on duty and authorized when AP-091 was issued. The clerk may record only the one update described in RTS-REQ-417 and may not add a physical control command.

The live evidence for the clerk's identity and role occupancy is the current authenticated-session record and active role-assignment record returned by the Workforce Identity and Access Service. The records must identify one clerk who is performing this gate and would submit the transaction, and the session and role records must identify that same clerk; the session record must state that the session is authenticated, and the role record must state that this clerk actively occupies the Maintenance Records Clerk role for NPO-7. The Access Administration Lead owns that service and has the remit to maintain authentication-session and role-assignment records. The live evidence for the clerk's on-duty status is the current duty assignment for that same authenticated clerk returned by the Maintenance Duty Roster service; it must state that the clerk is on duty for NPO-7 at gate time. The Maintenance Scheduling Lead owns that service and has the remit to maintain duty assignments. The live evidence for the safety precondition is the current asset-status record for EQ-204 returned by the Equipment Safety Hold Register service; it must identify EQ-204 and state that no open safety hold exists for EQ-204. The Safety Compliance Lead owns that service and has the remit to maintain safety-hold status for equipment.

The clerk obtains these three live records as part of the single pre-operation gate. A record qualifies only when it is returned for the one authenticated clerk who performs this gate and would submit the transaction, the Maintenance Records Clerk role assigned to that clerk for NPO-7, the duty assignment for that same clerk on duty for NPO-7, or asset EQ-204, as applicable to the check, and is current at the time the gate pass is recorded. A cached, stale, missing, unavailable, or contradictory response does not establish the required fact. If any of these records cannot be obtained or does not establish its required fact, the corresponding precondition fails; the clerk does not submit an entry and follows the gate-failure referral and safe-state rules in the return-to-service gate record.

AP-091 was signed in the approval register at 2026-09-12 14:00 UTC, after IN-552 was complete. The authorization becomes effective at that signing time. It ends when the one transaction completes, when the Reliability Supervisor records a withdrawal, or at 2026-09-12 23:59 UTC, whichever occurs first. The Reliability Supervisor is the only role allowed to withdraw it. A withdrawal is recorded as Withdrawal Notice WD-552 in the approval register and takes effect when that notice is recorded. The approval register, including AP-091 and WD-552, is the source used to establish validity and withdrawal. Its owner is the Reliability Supervisor, whose remit is approval and withdrawal decisions for this request.

If any listed precondition, credential, occupancy, or request-identity check fails, the clerk does not submit an entry and leaves the existing register status unchanged. The request is referred outside this class to the Reliability Supervisor, who owns the decision and may issue a new approval or close the request.

### 03-gate-and-exceptions.txt
Business record: return-to-service gate

The Maintenance Records Clerk performs the single pre-operation gate immediately before the register entry. The clerk must verify all of these facts and decisions: the authenticated user occupies the Maintenance Records Clerk role and is on duty; IN-552 is complete; AP-091 was granted by the Reliability Supervisor after that inspection, names EQ-204 and WO-7304, is still effective, and has no WD-552 withdrawal; EQ-204 has no open safety hold; the request concerns exactly one EQ-204 return-to-service entry; and no completed entry already exists for the same asset, work order, inspection, and approval tuple. The clerk records a gate pass only after every check is true. Under the existing AP-091 grant, this is fact-checking verification; it is not a new discretionary approval or a new grant.

For the live credential, role, duty, and safety-hold checks, the sources are the Workforce Identity and Access Service's current authenticated-session and active role-assignment records, the Maintenance Duty Roster service's current duty assignment, and the Equipment Safety Hold Register service's current asset-status record for EQ-204. The Access Administration Lead owns the Workforce Identity and Access Service and maintains authentication-session and role-assignment records. The Maintenance Scheduling Lead owns the Maintenance Duty Roster service and maintains duty assignments. The Safety Compliance Lead owns the Equipment Safety Hold Register service and maintains equipment safety-hold status. The clerk must obtain each record during this gate. The session and role records must identify one clerk who is performing this gate and would submit the transaction, and must identify that same clerk; the session record must state that the session is authenticated, and the role record must state that this clerk actively occupies the Maintenance Records Clerk role for NPO-7. The duty-roster record must identify that clerk and state that the clerk is on duty for NPO-7 at gate time. The safety-hold record must identify EQ-204 and state that no open safety hold exists for EQ-204. Each record must be current when the gate pass is recorded. A cached, stale, missing, unavailable, contradictory, or otherwise non-establishing response is an unproven check and causes the gate to fail.

If the gate fails, the safe response is to submit no transaction, issue no physical equipment command, and leave the existing Maintenance Register record unchanged. The external owner for an authority, scope, precondition, credential, occupancy, or gate failure is the Reliability Supervisor. A safety-hold issue is also sent to the Safety Compliance Lead through that owner. These referrals are outside the in-class path. Their destination is the Reliability Supervisor’s maintenance review queue; the safe state is no new return-to-service entry and no change to equipment; a request may return to this class only when the owner has provided a valid, unwithdrawn approval and all failed checks pass in a new review.

Any request to start, stop, move, energize, isolate, or physically control EQ-204 is outside this boundary. The destination for that request is the Operations Control Room Lead. The safe state is no physical command and no register update. It may return to this class only as a new maintenance-register request after the separate control procedure and a new approval are complete. Missing or contradictory business facts are referred in the same way; the packet contains no in-class branch for resolving them.

### 04-completion-evidence.txt
Business record: completion evidence for RTS-REQ-417

The exact native completion record is the Maintenance Register’s system-generated Return-to-Service Event. It must contain a unique event ID, event timestamp, asset EQ-204, work order WO-7304, inspection IN-552, approval AP-091, effective time 2026-09-12 14:30 UTC, performer role Maintenance Records Clerk, and reason RTS-AUTH-INSPECTED. It must show the resulting register status as Return to Service and link the event to the approval register entry. The Maintenance Register service creates the event and the Maintenance Records Clerk reads it after submission.

The clerk accepts completion only when the native event exists and every required value matches the request and source records. A missing event, a missing value, or any mismatch is completion failure. The clerk treats the current work instance as terminal: no second submission, replay, or physical command is allowed. The clerk leaves the prior register status in place and refers the failure outside this class to the Maintenance Records Custodian for record investigation and to the Reliability Supervisor for authority review. A later correction or attempt requires the authorities described in the duplicate and correction record.

The completion event is owned by the Maintenance Records Custodian. Its remit is to preserve the native register history, investigate missing or mismatched evidence, and authorize no semantic change without the required new approval. The event is evidence of the record update only; it is not evidence that EQ-204 was physically started, stopped, moved, energized, or isolated.

### 05-duplicates-corrections-and-policy.txt
Business record: duplicate, correction, and policy ownership rules

The same asset, work order, inspection, and approval tuple identifies one request instance. If the Maintenance Register already contains a completed Return-to-Service Event for that tuple, a duplicate request is rejected as a duplicate and creates no second event. A replay after a timeout or uncertain response is handled the same way: the clerk first relies on the native event lookup, and the current instance permits no additional submission or any equipment actuation. If no native event can be confirmed, the current instance remains terminal and the failure is referred for investigation.

The Maintenance Records Clerk has no authority to correct a completed event or to make another attempt under the same approval. A correction requires a Correction Authorization issued by the Reliability Supervisor, a correction event entered by the Maintenance Records Custodian linked to the original event, and a new approval when the effective business result must change. A later return-to-service attempt requires a new work order, new completed inspection, new approval, and a new request instance. The Reliability Supervisor may withdraw an approval only through Withdrawal Notice WD-552 in the approval register; withdrawal never permits a replay of the withdrawn instance.

Maintenance Control Standard MCS-12, revision 4, is the source for the policy choices governing the operation’s materiality, reversibility, preconditions, gate, completion evidence, duplicate rule, correction rule, and external boundaries. The Maintenance Governance Lead owns MCS-12 and has the remit to amend those policies. Delegation Register DR-08, revision 2, is the source for the Reliability Supervisor’s grant and withdrawal authority; the Maintenance Governance Lead also owns DR-08. The approval register is the source for the particular grant and withdrawal facts and is owned by the Reliability Supervisor. The maintenance work order, inspection, and approval records are the sources for the particular request facts and are owned by their named operational roles. The Maintenance Register is the source for completion and duplicate facts and is owned by the Maintenance Records Custodian.

Organizational attestation: the Maintenance Governance Lead attests that, under DR-08 revision 2, the Reliability Supervisor has the grant and withdrawal remit for this exact case scope: one return-to-service entry for equipment EQ-204 in its Maintenance Register record, using work order WO-7304, inspection IN-552, and approval AP-091. This attestation applies to RTS-REQ-417.

An amendment to MCS-12 or DR-08 requires a recorded policy amendment by the Maintenance Governance Lead and applies only to a new request instance. Withdrawal or suspension of a policy choice also requires a recorded policy withdrawal notice issued by the Maintenance Governance Lead; the notice is the source for that withdrawal, and the lead owns it and has the remit to withdraw or amend the policy. No policy choice in this packet is awaiting decision. The named owners and their remits are the complete route for amendment, withdrawal, correction, and exception handling.

### Revision 3 fixture amendments

The following source amendments are part of this repository-owned revision 3 fixture. They close source gaps identified during translation review without changing the original revision2-source.txt file.

1. The Maintenance Register provider enforces atomic uniqueness for the tuple `{asset, work_order, inspection, approval}`. It supports authoritative lookup by that tuple and by generated event ID. A successful write commits only if the current approval-register revision still shows AP-091 as effective and unwithdrawn under the dispatch fence.
2. The pre-operation gate requires four independent live records: the authenticated session, the active role assignment, the current duty assignment, and the current safety-hold status. The session and role records are separate records even when returned by one service.
3. Each live record is queried at the exact gate instant. Its provider revision must assert applicability at that instant. Cached or locally inferred records do not satisfy the gate.
4. The AP-091 expiry at 2026-09-12 23:59 UTC is exclusive. The effective interval is `[2026-09-12T14:00:00Z, 2026-09-12T23:59:00Z)`.
5. The completion result fields are exactly asset, work order, inspection, approval, effective time, and resulting status. A change to any of those fields requires a new approval basis. Performer role, reason, event identifier, timestamp, and approval-register link require correction authority when changed but do not by themselves require a new approval basis.
6. An amendment to MCS-12 or DR-08 applies only to a new request instance. A policy withdrawal blocks an undispatched instance through the provider dispatch fence and does not rewrite prior history.
